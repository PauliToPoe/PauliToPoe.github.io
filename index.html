About

Hello fellow humans and literate animals. This website was borne from the mind of a Master's physics student looking for expression. Physics, with all it's magnificent beauty, is a different kind of beauty to that found in literature which seeks to convey beauty through illustriuos descriptions, through events in a story that evoke or perhaps through the altered lens that you see the universe through after a good book. This kind of beauty, is what makes people individual. I have spent too long with physics as my sole source of beauty; countless manipulations of Pauli matrices. It is time I taste the other kind of beauty. So, welcome to my website, if anything is useful to you in someway I am grateful, join me on my journey from Pauli to Poe.

For Whom The Bell Tolls - Ernest Hemingway - Read 04/2026

In 1940, Hemingway decided to publish a novel on the 1936-39 Spanish civil war fought between the socialist Republicans and the right-wing Nationalists. A book on war is nothing new; it provides a natural (depending on your view of humanity) setting to elicit the strongest emotions felt by humans. This is amplified in For Whom The Bell Tolls (FWTBT) in us following the main character Robert Jordan who has volunteered to fight in the war whilst coming from the US. Whatever Robert has to win, lose or watch idly, is not for his survival. He has injected himself into a foreign war, prepared to be robbed of every experience or emotion there is to be felt in this fleeting moment of time, on this tiny planet, we call life. The outcome of this war, will have little impact on his daily life back in the US where he his a professor teaching Spanish, yet he is prepared to lose everything. This is the ultimate devotion to ones beliefs, provided it is thought of through a sane, uncorrupted mind. I think this sentiment, of how devoted Robert is to his beliefs is easily overlooked in todays world. I have been living in London for the past 9 months where the average person, regardless of background, will not even say 'Thank you' to a bus driver after assisting them in getting to an important meeting on time or allowing them the freedom of travel around these concrete structures we sit in for 8 hours a day. People have lost the ability to remove themselves from their daily lives and think of themselves as part of a larger organism, one which, if to be healthy, requires a concsious effort from those within to recognise and act upon the philosphies capable vastly improving society. Perhaps the government should place more emphasis on making people see the logic of these philosphies. People talking between themselves would replace the need for billboards. I do not think communist Russia was good at all, and although it may be a fallacy (probably is), I always imagine the Russians of being able to look past the personal hardships they faced, forego the need for luxury, and look towards the common collective good of their people. Wouldn't it be nice nowadays. One problem is trust, the average person, will be happy to share with their family, or romantic partner; they would be happy to share with a wider group including all their friends. But they would not be happy to share with their neighbour whom they do not speak to. I believe this is linked to the ease of travel and employment abroad, mixed in with division, coming in many forms such as racism and classism. We no longer live in a town familiar with all inhabitants from birth. We are a mixed society, with many different groups having different beliefs. I believe this community thinking, may be driving a wedge between these different groups, preventing us from recognising the common good we should be fighting for. Now to get back to the book. As previously said, this is a book about war. So why is it that the firt time the main character is involved in combat is 7/8 through the book? War is an exciting setting for many readers and film lovers, allowing flaws in acting or writing to be washed away by a grand clash of armies. Hemingway does none of this.
